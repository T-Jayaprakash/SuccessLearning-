OET (Occupational English Test)

The Occupational English Test (OET) is a language proficiency test designed for healthcare professionals who seek to work in English-speaking healthcare environments. OET assesses the English language skills of professionals such as nurses, doctors, dentists, pharmacists, and other healthcare workers.

Key points about the OET

The OET consists of four sub-tests, which assess the four language skills: Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking. Each of these sub-tests is designed to reflect real healthcare scenarios.

The content of a spoken English course typically includes vocabulary building, pronunciation, listening and speaking exercises, and conversations in real-life situations. Courses may focus on different accents, idiomatic expressions, and business English if required.

OET is primarily intended for healthcare professionals, including but not limited to doctors, nurses, dentists, pharmacists, physiotherapists, radiographers, and occupational therapists. It may vary slightly in content depending on the specific profession.

OET is scored on a scale from A (highest) to E (lowest) for each sub-test. Candidates receive a separate score for each sub-test, and the overall OET grade is determined by the combined performance across all sub-tests.

The test content is tailored to the healthcare sector. For example, the reading and listening materials are healthcare-related, and the writing and speaking tasks are healthcare-specific.
