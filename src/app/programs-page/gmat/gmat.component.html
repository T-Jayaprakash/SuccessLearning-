GMAT (Graduate Management Admission Test)

The GMAT, or Graduate Management Admission Test, is a standardized test used for admissions into graduate business and management programs, primarily MBA (Master of Business Administration) programs. The GMAT assesses a test-taker's abilities in various areas related to business and management. The GMAT is administered online and in standardized test centers in 114 countries around the world. The number of test-takers of GMAT plummeted from 2012 to 2021 as more students opted for an MBA programs that didn't require the GMAT.

Who can take the GMAT test?

There are no particular academic qualifications specified for GMAT takers. Most of the GMAT aspirants are either graduate or studying in final year of graduation, or working professionals. It is noteworthy that regardless of your graduation stream or other academic qualifications, you can go for the exam.

What is GMAT exam used for?

The Graduate Management Admission Test is a graduate admissions entrance exam designed specifically for students who want to go to graduate school for business. A GMAT score is often required when submitting a graduate program application for an MBA, Master of Accountancy, or Master of Finance.

Key points about the GMAT

1. Sections: The GMAT consists of four main sections:
- Analytical Writing Assessment (AWA): Requires test-takers to write an essay analyzing an argument. This section measures critical thinking and analytical writing skills.
- Integrated Reasoning (IR): Assesses the ability to integrate data from various sources, including graphs, tables, and text, to solve complex problems.
- Quantitative Reasoning: Tests mathematical and quantitative skills, including data sufficiency and problem- solving.
- Verbal Reasoning: Measures reading comprehension, critical reasoning, and sentence correction skills.

2. Scores: The GMAT is scored on a scale of 200 to 800, in 10-point increments. The AWA section is scored separately on a scale of 0 to 6, in half-point increments.

3. Test Format: The GMAT is administered in a computer-based format. Test-takers receive unofficial scores for the verbal and quantitative sections immediately after completing the test, while official scores, including AWA and IR, are available later.

4. Registration: You can register for the GMAT online through the official GMAT website. You'll need to create a GMAT account, select a test date and location, and pay the registration fee.

5. Preparation: Many test-takers prepare for the GMAT by using study materials, taking practice tests, and potentially enrolling in test preparation courses. There are various test prep books, online resources, and courses available to help you prepare.

Preparing for the GMAT requires a combination of test-taking strategies and content knowledge. You should research the admission requirements of the business schools you plan to apply to and tailor your preparation accordingly. Good luck with your GMAT preparations and test-taking!
